When to lead trump in euchre

Leading trump is one of the strongest plays in euchre, and one of the easiest to get wrong. One simple idea sorts out most hands.

The one rule

Lead trump when it leaves your side ahead after the trump is gone.

That happens in two ways. Your side will be the last one still holding trump. Or you hold off suit winners, like an ace, and pulling the other team's trump leaves nothing that can beat them.

A trump lead pulls trump from everyone who has it, and that includes your partner. So before you lead it, ask which side runs out first.

Good times to lead trump

These are the common ones when your team called trump:

  • You are opening the hand for the calling side.
  • Your partner called trump and it has not been led yet.
  • You hold the highest trump left, so nothing can beat your lead.
  • You hold enough trump that your side will be the last one with any.
  • Your partner picked up the right bower. A low trump lead makes the other team spend trump while your partner waits behind the play with the best card in the game.

Lead high or lead low?

If you hold the highest trump still out, lead it. If a higher trump is still out, a low trump is usually the safer lead. You give up a small card to pull the big ones, and the trump you kept moves up in rank.

There are two exceptions.

  • Touching cards. If your two highest trump sit next to each other in rank, lead the higher one. The classic case is the left bower with the ace of trump behind it. If the right bower takes your left, your ace is now the highest trump left.
  • Two tricks against you. When the other team already has two tricks, the next one they win euchres your side. Stop pulling trump with small cards and lead your highest to take the sure trick.

When to hold back

  • It is your last trump. Even a trump that is sure to win is usually worth more later. Take your safe tricks first.
  • There is nothing left to pull. Once the other team has shown they are out of trump, a trump lead only spends cards your side may still need.
  • Your trump is small and you have no control. Without a bower or the highest trump left, your lead mostly loses to the bigger trump still out. Keep it to trump in later, and lead an off suit card for now.
  • An ace is waiting. If you hold an off suit ace in a short suit, play it before more trump is spent. The ace can be trumped if you wait.

On defense

When the other team called trump, leading it is usually risky. It removes the trump your side needs to take tricks later, and it pulls your partner's trump along with theirs.

It can be right when you believe the callers are low on trump, you hold several trump yourself, or you may hold the highest trump still out. A trump lead on defense should be rare and chosen on purpose.

The answer can change during a hand

Early in a hand, holding your trump back can be right. A few tricks later, leading it can become right. The rule did not change. The situation did: you have taken tricks you did not have before, or the trump above yours has been played and yours is now the best one left.

Rule of thumb: Lead trump when it leaves your side ahead. If your trump is weak and nothing is waiting behind it, look for an off suit ace or a short suit lead.
